Life Events

One-time money out (or gifts) at a specific age — home purchase, car, medical care, inheritance, and more
Three related tools here, each for a different pattern of money. Life Events (this card) are a single one-time hit — money leaves your portfolio exactly once, in the year you choose. Additional Income and Additional Expenses (below) are the opposite: a recurring amount every year for a limited stretch you define with a start age and a number of years — a board seat for 10 years, medical costs from age 65 for 15 years, and so on. Use Life Events for a single moment; use the recurring lists for anything ongoing but not permanent.

Sensitivity: What if growth is higher or lower?

Stress-test your plan against market surprises
This slider shifts your growth rates up or down by the amount you choose — the middle value (0%) uses your rates exactly as entered. It also sets the width of the conservative–optimistic range shown on the Growth & Runway chart. That range doesn't stay at full width forever: it's strongest for about the first 15 years, then gradually narrows toward roughly a third of its original width, since real markets don't hold a fixed edge for decades on end. Your Year-by-year table always reflects the middle (expected) case.

Portfolio Growth & Retirement Runway

Your full lifetime in one chart — hover to see your balance at any age. Axis switches to log only when the shown range gets too wide for linear to stay readable
The top panel is your balance: rust while you're still working and saving, red once you're withdrawing in retirement. The shaded band is the conservative-to-optimistic range from your Sensitivity setting. The bottom panel shows the actual cash moving each year — green bars for investment return, red bars for whatever's going out (expenses or withdrawal), and a dashed grey line for your full cost of living. Drag "Show chart through age" to focus on a shorter window — the axis will automatically switch back to linear once the range is narrow enough to read comfortably, and to log if you stretch it out across the full decades-long horizon.
